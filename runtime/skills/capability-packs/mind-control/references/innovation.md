# Innovation: Radical Candidates, Falsifiable Claims

Read for requested invention, strategic R&D or a demonstrated limitation of existing approaches. Preserve room for unconventional solutions without forcing novelty.

## Four useful transformations

1. **Assumption inversion:** Select one consequential assumption, classify it as physical/logical, contractual, policy-bound or conventional, and ask what changes if it is relaxed. Do not invert safety or authority requirements. State what evidence makes relaxation plausible.
2. **Anomaly mining:** Inspect repeatable outliers, friction and unexplained failures. Check instrumentation, sampling and confounders before treating an anomaly as a new mechanism. A noisy measurement is not a mandate for a redesign.
3. **Counter-strategy:** Construct the strongest credible alternative to the conventional route. Compare both on the same constraints and evidence. Consensus is neither proof nor grounds for rejection; a 10% improvement may be the best outcome.
4. **First-principles reconstruction:** Express the desired result without naming the current tools. Rebuild a candidate route from hard constraints, then reintroduce migration cost, compatibility, human needs and operational ownership. “Start from zero” is an analytical exercise, not permission to discard working systems.

## Transfer a mechanism, not an analogy

Identify a donor field and its actual causal or structural mechanism. Map donor inputs, outputs, states, invariants and failure conditions to the target. State where the analogy breaks and which observation would falsify the transfer. A resemblance in terminology or appearance is not functional equivalence.

For combinations, specify what each mechanism supplies and consumes. Check incompatible assumptions, feedback loops, resource contention, state ownership and failure propagation. Explain the additional capability the combination might enable and compare it with one simpler mechanism. Mark the result as a hypothesis until tested.

## Ten-year vision and backcasting

Describe a possible future capability separately from demonstrated behavior. Remove optional inherited constraints for exploration, not legal, safety or user requirements. Backcast prerequisites until one small reversible experiment is identifiable now. Name the unknown that experiment resolves, a measurable discriminator, likely cost, authority required and a stopping rule. Do not create an implementation roadmap beyond the user's requested scope.

## Multi-objective comparison

Use criteria that matter to this decision: outcome fit, evidence, reliability, latency, resource/token cost, integration effort, reversibility, maintainability, scalability and cognitive load. Separate hard gates from preferences. Use measured values where available and label estimates/unknowns. Add weights only when their rationale is clear; do not manufacture precision. Check whether small changes in assumptions reverse the ranking. A dominated option need not survive merely to fill a table.

## Candidate card

```text
Outcome and current limitation:
Conventional baseline / minimal fix:
Assumption challenged:
Proposed mechanism and transferred structure:
Inputs, outputs, state and contracts:
Why it might improve the outcome:
Strongest objection and failure modes:
Where the analogy or model breaks:
Smallest reversible discriminator:
Evidence that would reject it:
Permission, cost and stopping conditions:
Status: proposal / inspected / tested, with evidence.
```

Do not claim “unassailable,” “optimal,” “deterministic cognition” or an implemented capability from persuasive prose. Revisit a conclusion when evidence changes; do not endlessly revisit settled decisions without a material reason.

## Value and anti-goals

Define user value, acceptance and what must not get worse before scoring candidates. Quantify ROI only when benefits, costs and time horizon are grounded; otherwise report conditional estimates, ranges or qualitative value. Include memory overhead versus context retention when relevant. Keep measurement noise, sampling bias and proxy-metric failure visible. A weighted score cannot compensate for a failed hard constraint.
